How the licence is checked

Your licence file is signed by us, and Solander verifies that signature on your Mac against a key built into the app. Separately, a helper process that runs alongside the app — not the app that opens your documents, which has no network access at all — confirms with us about once a week that the purchase behind your licence still stands. It sends your licence ID, the app version and a random install ID. It does not send, and cannot send, anything about your documents.

If that check cannot reach us, the licensed features keep working for a grace period of 30 days after the last successful check. A newly installed licence works for 60 days before a first check is needed. If the grace period runs out, the licensed features pause until the next successful check; they are not deleted, and nothing you have made stops working.

For a Mac that is never allowed on a network, there is offline activation: the app shows a request code, you enter it on a web page from another device, and enter the signed response code back into the app. A first activation lasts 15 days and each renewal lasts 90, and renewal is the same exchange again.

What we store, and for how long, is in the privacy notice.

Setting your Mac’s clock backwards to extend a time-limited activation does not work, and Solander will pause the licensed features if it detects it. Putting the clock right, or letting one check succeed, restores them.

What Solander does not promise

Solander does not determine that a document is anonymous, lawful to disclose, or safe to publish. It helps you remove information from documents and reports on what it did. Detection is best-effort and misses content, particularly names, addresses and non-Latin text. A passing verification means no readable text remains inside the regions that were redacted — nothing more.

Deciding what must be removed, and checking that it was, remains yours. Solander is a tool for that work, not a substitute for it, and it is not legal advice. Reports Solander produces are records of what the software did; they are not certifications, and we do not certify any document.
